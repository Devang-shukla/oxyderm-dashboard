How Many Laser Hair Removal Sessions Do You Really Need? (Edmonton Guide)

Published: August 2026 | Reading time: 7 minutes

If you're researching laser hair removal in Edmonton, you've probably seen clinics advertise "permanent hair reduction" but wonder how many sessions it actually takes to get there. The answer isn't as simple as a single number—your treatment timeline depends on your unique hair growth cycles, skin tone, and the area being treated.

At Oxyderm Laser Clinic in Edmonton, we've helped thousands of clients achieve smooth, hair-free skin. Based on our experience, most people need 6-8 sessions to see 70-90% permanent hair reduction. But let's break down why that number varies and what YOU can expect.

---

Why Session Numbers Vary (It's Not One-Size-Fits-All)

Laser hair removal works by targeting the melanin (pigment) in your hair follicle during its active growth phase. Here's the catch: not all your hair is growing at the same time. At any given moment, only 20-30% of your hair is in the "anagen" (active growth) phase—the only stage where laser treatment is effective.

This is why you need multiple sessions: each treatment catches a new batch of hair entering its growth phase.

Hair Growth Cycles Explained

Your hair goes through three phases:

1. Anagen (Growth Phase): Hair is actively growing. Laser works here. 2. Catagen (Transition Phase): Hair stops growing, follicle shrinks. Laser doesn't work. 3. Telogen (Resting Phase): Hair falls out, follicle is dormant. Laser doesn't work.

Different body areas have different cycle lengths:

  • Face: 4-week cycles (faster regrowth)
  • Underarms, bikini: 6-8 week cycles
  • Legs, back: 8-12 week cycles (slower regrowth)
This is why we space sessions 6-8 weeks apart—to catch each new growth wave.

Skin Tone + Hair Color Factors

Laser hair removal works best when there's high contrast between your skin tone and hair color:

  • Dark hair + light skin: Ideal candidates, fastest results (often 6 sessions)
  • Dark hair + darker skin: Requires specialized lasers (Nd:YAG), may need 7-9 sessions
  • Light/blonde/red hair + any skin: Lower melanin in hair makes it harder to target, may need 10+ sessions or may not be a good candidate

Treatment Area Differences

Some body areas respond faster than others:

  • Fast responders (6-7 sessions): Underarms, bikini line, face
  • Moderate (7-8 sessions): Full legs, arms, chest
  • Slower (8-10 sessions): Back, shoulders (especially for men with hormonal hair growth)

Factors That Change Your Timeline

Hormonal Conditions (PCOS, Menopause)

If you have polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) or hormonal imbalances, you may need:

  • 9-12 sessions initially
  • Annual maintenance sessions (1-2/year) to address hormone-triggered regrowth
PCOS causes elevated androgens (male hormones) that continuously stimulate new hair growth, even after laser treatment.

Medication Effects

Some medications can interfere with results or require special precautions:

  • Hormonal birth control: Can slow results if recently started/stopped
  • Accutane (isotretinoin): Must wait 6 months after stopping before laser
  • Blood thinners: May increase bruising risk
  • Photosensitizing drugs: Can cause burns (tell your technician!)

Age + Metabolism

  • Younger clients (under 25): May need more sessions due to active hormone production
  • Peri-menopause/menopause: Hormonal shifts can cause new growth, requiring maintenance

Spacing Between Sessions: The 6-8 Week Rule

Can I do sessions every 4 weeks to finish faster?

No—and here's why it backfires:

Laser only works on hair in the anagen (growth) phase. If you treat too frequently, you're zapping the same hair over and over instead of waiting for new hair to enter its growth phase. You'll waste money and time without better results.

FAQ: Your Top Questions About Session Numbers

Can I do sessions every 4 weeks to finish faster?

No. Hair growth cycles dictate timing, not urgency. Treating every 4 weeks means you're re-treating the same hair instead of catching new growth. Stick to 6-8 weeks for best results.

What if I miss a session or take a 3-month break?

No problem! You won't lose progress. The hair you've already treated is gone. When you return, you'll just continue targeting remaining hair.

Do men need more sessions than women?

Often, yes—especially for back, shoulders, and chest. Men's hair tends to be:

  • Coarser (more melanin = better laser response, but deeper follicles)
  • Denser (more hair per square inch)
  • Hormonally driven (testosterone stimulates regrowth)
Men typically need 7-9 sessions vs. women's 6-8 sessions for the same area.

Can I shave between sessions?

Yes! In fact, you SHOULD shave 24 hours before each session. Shaving is fine because it doesn't remove the hair follicle (the laser's target). Avoid waxing, plucking, or threading, which remove the follicle and make laser ineffective.

What if I only want 50% reduction, not 90%?

Some clients (especially men) prefer to keep some hair for a natural look. You can stop treatment anytime you're happy with results. Many stop after 4-5 sessions for a "thinned out" look instead of complete removal.

Laser Hair Removal Before and After: What Edmonton Clients Actually Experience

Most people walk into their first laser hair removal appointment unsure of what to expect. The before-and-after photos online look great. The process itself? Less explained.

---

Before Your First Appointment

Preparation matters more than most clinics tell you. Skipping prep steps is the most common reason results take longer than expected.

Shave — Do Not Wax

Shave the treatment area 24 hours before your session. Not the day of (skin needs time to recover from irritation), and not earlier than 24 hours (hair needs to be just below the surface).

Do not wax, thread, or epilate in the four weeks leading up to your appointment. Laser targets the pigment in the hair follicle. If the follicle is empty — as it is after waxing — the laser has nothing to work with.

Avoid Sun Exposure

Stay out of direct sun and skip tanning products for at least two weeks before each session. Tanned or recently sun-exposed skin has elevated melanin, which increases the risk of pigment changes after treatment.

This applies to self-tanner, spray tan, and tanning beds as well.

Arrive Clean

Come to your appointment with clean, dry skin. No lotions, creams, deodorant, or perfume on the area being treated. These products can interfere with the laser and increase sensitivity.

Numbing Cream (Optional)

For sensitive areas — upper lip, bikini line, underarms — you can apply a topical numbing cream 30 to 45 minutes before your session. Ask us about this at your consultation.

Does Laser Hair Removal Work on All Skin Types? Edmonton Clinic Explains

Published: August 2026 | Reading time: 7 minutes

If you have darker skin, you've probably encountered conflicting information about laser hair removal. Some websites say "safe for all skin tones!" while others warn "not recommended for dark skin." So which is it?

The truth is more nuanced: Laser hair removal CAN work on all skin types—but not all lasers are safe for all skin tones. Using the wrong technology on darker skin can cause burns, hyperpigmentation, or ineffective results.

At Oxyderm Laser Clinic in Edmonton, we treat clients across the full Fitzpatrick scale (skin types I-VI), from very fair to very dark skin. Here's what you need to know about laser hair removal for YOUR skin tone—and how to avoid clinics that can't safely treat you.

---

Understanding the Fitzpatrick Skin Type Scale

Before we dive into laser safety, let's identify YOUR skin type. Dermatologists use the Fitzpatrick Scale (I-VI) to classify skin based on melanin levels and how it reacts to sun exposure:

TypeDescriptionSun ReactionLaser Considerations
IVery fair, pale whiteAlways burns, never tansIdeal for most lasers
IIFair, whiteUsually burns, tans minimallyIdeal for most lasers
IIIMedium, light brownSometimes burns, tans graduallySafe with most lasers
IVOlive, moderate brownRarely burns, tans easilyNeeds longer-wavelength lasers
VBrown, dark brownVery rarely burns, tans very easilyRequires Nd:YAG laser
VIVery dark brown to blackNever burns, deeply pigmentedRequires Nd:YAG laser + expert technician

Why does skin type matter for laser hair removal?

Laser hair removal works by targeting melanin (pigment). The laser light is absorbed by melanin in the hair follicle, heating it until it's destroyed. But here's the challenge: melanin is ALSO in your skin.

  • Light skin (Types I-III): High contrast between skin and hair = laser easily targets hair, not skin
  • Dark skin (Types IV-VI): Melanin in skin competes with melanin in hair = risk of laser burning skin instead of follicle
This is NOT a limitation of dark skin—it's a limitation of OLDER laser technology. Modern lasers (when used correctly) work beautifully on darker skin tones.

---

The 3 Main Laser Types (And Which Work for Dark Skin)

Not all "laser hair removal" uses the same laser. There are three main types used in Edmonton clinics, and only ONE is truly safe for dark skin.

1. Alexandrite Laser (755nm Wavelength)

Best for: Fitzpatrick I-III (light to medium skin) NOT safe for: Fitzpatrick IV-VI (darker skin)

How it works:

  • Short wavelength (755nm) = absorbed quickly by melanin
  • Very fast treatment times (legs in 20 minutes)
  • Highly effective for light skin + dark hair
Why it's risky for dark skin:
  • Short wavelength can't differentiate between skin melanin and hair melanin
  • High risk of burns, blistering, hyperpigmentation (dark spots)
  • Some clinics still use this on Type IV skin "on low settings"—DON'T let them. It's not worth the risk.

2. Diode Laser (800-810nm Wavelength)

Best for: Fitzpatrick I-IV (light to olive skin) Questionable for: Fitzpatrick V-VI (darker skin—depends on settings and technician skill)

How it works:

  • Medium wavelength (800-810nm) = penetrates deeper than alexandrite
  • Good balance of speed + safety for light-to-medium skin
  • Can sometimes treat Type IV-V skin with conservative settings
Why it's still risky for very dark skin:
  • Wavelength is borderline for high melanin levels
  • Requires VERY experienced technician to adjust fluence (energy) correctly
  • Some dark-skinned clients report good results, others get burns—it's inconsistent
At Oxyderm, we use diode lasers for Fitzpatrick I-IV clients, but we do NOT push it on Type V-VI clients. If you're darker than olive, we use Nd:YAG (see below).

3. Nd:YAG Laser (1064nm Wavelength) — THE GOLD STANDARD FOR DARK SKIN

Best for: Fitzpatrick IV-VI (olive to very dark skin) Also safe for: Fitzpatrick I-III (but slower than alexandrite/diode)

How it works:

  • Long wavelength (1064nm) = bypasses melanin in the skin, targets ONLY the hair follicle
  • Lower risk of burns or hyperpigmentation
  • Slightly longer treatment times (because it uses lower energy to stay safe)
Why it's the safest choice for dark skin:
  • FDA-approved specifically for dark skin (Fitzpatrick IV-VI)
  • Used by dermatologists worldwide for melanin-rich skin
  • Lower risk of side effects when operated by a trained technician
The trade-off:
  • Treatments take 20-30% longer than alexandrite
  • May require 7-9 sessions instead of 6-8 (because lower energy = more gradual results)
  • Costs slightly more at some clinics (though NOT at Oxyderm—we charge the same rate)
At Oxyderm, we use Nd:YAG technology for all Fitzpatrick V-VI clients and offer it as an option for Type IV clients who want maximum safety.

---

What Can Go Wrong with the Wrong Laser on Dark Skin?

Using alexandrite or high-powered diode lasers on dark skin can cause:

1. Burns and Blistering

When the laser can't differentiate between skin melanin and hair melanin, it heats BOTH. Result:

  • First-degree burns (redness, pain for 2-3 days)
  • Second-degree burns (blistering, open wounds, scarring risk)
Real client story (from another Edmonton clinic, came to Oxyderm for correction): "I'm Filipina (Type IV skin). I went to a cheaper clinic and they used their 'regular laser' on low settings. My legs blistered within hours. I had dark marks for 6 months. When I came to Oxyderm, they explained I needed Nd:YAG. Zero issues since."

2. Hyperpigmentation (Dark Spots)

Even if you don't get burns, using the wrong laser can trigger post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH)—dark patches where the laser hit.

This is VERY common in South Asian, Black, and Latino clients treated with alexandrite or diode lasers.

How to avoid it:

  • Use Nd:YAG laser ONLY
  • Test patch on a small area 48 hours before full treatment
  • Avoid sun exposure 2 weeks before treatment (tan = more melanin = higher burn risk)

3. Ineffective Results (Wasted Money)

Some clinics turn laser settings SO low (to avoid burning dark skin) that the treatment becomes ineffective. You pay full price, but the follicle isn't damaged enough to stop regrowth.

Red flag: If your technician says "we'll just use really low settings on you," find a clinic with Nd:YAG instead.

Microneedling vs Laser Hair Removal: Comparing Outcomes for Edmonton Clients

Two of the most requested treatments at Oxyderm are laser hair removal and microneedling. They are completely different procedures with completely different goals — but clients often ask us which one they should do, or whether they can do both.

The short answer: they address different problems. The longer answer is below.

---

What Each Treatment Actually Does

Laser Hair Removal

Laser hair removal uses concentrated light energy to target pigment in the hair follicle. The heat damages the follicle, interrupting its ability to grow new hair.

It does not affect skin texture, collagen, or surface quality. Its only goal is permanent reduction of unwanted hair.

Microneedling

Microneedling uses a device with fine needles to create controlled micro-channels in the skin's surface. This triggers the body's wound-healing response — producing collagen and elastin to repair the tiny injuries.

The result over time: improved skin texture, reduced appearance of acne scars, tighter pores, smoother surface, and softer fine lines.

Microneedling does not remove hair. It does not treat follicles. It treats the skin itself.

What the Treatment Feels Like

Laser Hair Removal

The sensation is a quick, sharp pulse — described by most clients as a rubber band snap. The device moves across the treatment area in a consistent pattern. Sessions are brief: underarms take 10 to 15 minutes, full legs 45 to 60 minutes.

Sensitivity varies by area. The Brazilian and upper lip are the most sensitive zones. Lower legs and back are generally well-tolerated.

A cooling mechanism in the device reduces discomfort during treatment.

Microneedling

Microneedling on the face involves the application of a topical numbing cream before treatment, which takes 20 to 30 minutes to take effect. Once numb, the device moves across the skin in overlapping passes. Most clients feel pressure and mild warmth rather than sharp pain.

After the session, the face is red — similar in appearance to a moderate sunburn. This fades over 24 to 48 hours.

Microneedling is more intensive than laser hair removal in terms of downtime and post-care requirements.

---

Recovery and Downtime

Laser Hair Removal

Minimal. Redness and warmth for a few hours. Most clients return to normal activity the same day. Avoid direct sun exposure and heavy exercise for 24 to 48 hours.

Microneedling

More noticeable. Redness and some skin sensitivity lasting 24 to 72 hours. Skin may feel tight and dry for several days. You will need to avoid active skincare ingredients (retinol, AHAs, BHAs), sun exposure, and makeup for a defined recovery period.

Post-care is more involved: gentle cleanser, fragrance-free moisturizer, SPF 30+ whenever outdoors.
