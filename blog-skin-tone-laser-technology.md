# Why Your Skin Tone Dictates Your Laser Hair Removal Technology

*By Hetisha Shukla, Lead Medical Esthetician at Oxyderm Laser Clinic*

One of the most persistent myths in aesthetics is that "laser hair removal only works on fair skin and dark hair." 

While this was true in the late 1990s, medical laser physics has evolved dramatically. Today, anyone can safely achieve permanent hair reduction—**provided your practitioner is using the correct laser wavelength for your specific Fitzpatrick skin type.**

If a clinic uses the wrong laser on melanin-rich skin, the consequences can be devastating: superficial burns, scarring, and severe hyperpigmentation. 

Here is what you must know about how laser wavelengths interact with melanin, and why your skin tone determines the laser technology required.

---

## 1. Understanding the Fitzpatrick Skin Scale

In clinical dermatology, skin is classified from Type I to Type VI based on melanin content and reaction to ultraviolet light:

* **Fitzpatrick Type I–II:** Very fair to fair skin, light eyes, burns easily, rarely tans.
* **Fitzpatrick Type III–IV:** Medium to olive skin, beige/light brown, tans easily (common in Mediterranean, Hispanic, and East Asian backgrounds).
* **Fitzpatrick Type V–VI:** Deep brown to rich dark skin, rarely burns, produces abundant melanin (common in South Asian, Middle Eastern, Afro-Caribbean, and African backgrounds).

---

## 2. How Laser Hair Removal Targets the Follicle

Lasers work through a principle called **Selective Photothermolysis**. The laser emits a specific wavelength of light absorbed by the **melanin (pigment)** in the hair shaft. This light converts into heat, cauterizing the blood supply at the base of the hair follicle without damaging the surrounding tissue.

### The Challenge with Melanin-Rich Skin (Types IV–VI):
The melanin in the skin and the melanin in the hair follicle compete for the laser's energy. 
* If a short wavelength (like a standard 755nm Alexandrite or 810nm Diode) is fired into deep skin at high energy, the skin's surface absorbs too much heat before the light reaches the deep follicle root. This causes surface burns.

---

## 3. The Solution: Long-Pulsed 1064nm Nd:YAG Laser

For Fitzpatrick Types IV, V, and VI, the **1064nm Nd:YAG laser** is the uncontested gold standard worldwide:

* **Deep Penetration:** The 1064nm wavelength has the deepest tissue penetration of any aesthetic laser. It bypasses the epidermal melanin in the top layer of skin and deposits its thermal energy directly into the bulb of the hair follicle deep in the dermis.
* **Melanin-Safe:** Because it has a lower absorption coefficient in melanin, it treats Black, Brown, and tanned skin with maximum safety and zero risk of discoloration.
